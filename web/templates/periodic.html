How the evidence is assessed Repeatability is a diagnostic score, not Gaussian significance: every time split must score at least 5, with support in at least 75% of its intervals. At least three DM trials and eight samples per period are required. Near-zero DM, a strong DM-zero response, and disagreement across the band defer a fold. Missing or invalid time diagnostics also defer it. This conservative shortlist can defer weak or intermittent sources.
Hidden by default: periods found in several beams. A period that shows up in 4 or more beams, or in two SAPs, of one observation is RFI. The exception is when every fold of it sits at one DM above zero, as a bright pulsar in neighbouring beams would. Tick multi-beam to see them.
Reading a fold. A pulsar has a narrow pulse, stays at one phase through the hour, is present across the band, and peaks at a DM above zero. RFI tends to be broad, intermittent, confined to some channels, or strongest at DM 0, and often shows up at the same period in many beams. τscat is the median scatter broadening expected at this DM at 135 MHz (Bhat et al. 2004). Real lines of sight vary about tenfold either way. When τscat exceeds the period, a real pulsar would fold to a flat line. Fold χ²/dof uses the whole trial's variance: red noise pushes it below 1 at short periods and far above 1 at long ones. Compare it only between candidates of similar period.
